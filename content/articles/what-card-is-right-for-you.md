## Why do you actually want a credit card?

If you’re only using it to spend money you don’t have, or you don’t plan to use the benefits, why pay for getting a credit card? You could simply use your **debit card** instead.

And if your main reason is just to split a big purchase into smaller payments, an **instalment plan or Buy Now, Pay Later** option may make more sense depending on the fees and terms.

### A credit card is worth considering when you can pay it off responsibly and actually use the rewards and benefits you’re paying for.

Otherwise, you could end up **paying for a card every year but barely using what it gives you.**

So before you apply, think about these two things:

## 1. How much do you spend each month?

First, look at your **average monthly spending**.

Are you spending $1,000, $3,000, $5,000 or $10,000+ a month?

This matters because the amount you spend can make a big difference to how much value you get from a card.

For example, a card with a **$700 annual fee** might not make sense if you only spend a small amount each month. But if you put a lot of your regular expenses on the card, the points, travel benefits and other perks may make the fee worthwhile.

Also look at **where your money goes:** groceries, dining, bills, shopping, travel and everyday purchases. Some cards reward certain types of spending much better than others.

### How you spend is the more important factor in choosing the right earn rate and rewards structure.

## 2. What is your lifestyle?

Before choosing a card, think about **how you actually spend and travel**.

Do you travel overseas often?  
Travel mostly around Australia?  
Travel as a couple or family?  
Want airport lounge access?  
Fly with Qantas, Virgin or book a trip with an online agent?  
Do you mainly spend on groceries, dining, shopping and everyday expenses?

**There’s no one-size-fits-all credit card.** A card that’s great for frequent travellers may be a waste of money if you rarely travel. The best card is the one that fits **your lifestyle, spending, and travel habits.**

### The best credit card isn’t necessarily the one with the most points — it’s the one that fits your spending and lifestyle.

The biggest earn rate is not automatically the best deal. A useful comparison starts with where you already spend, how you redeem points, and which fees you will actually pay.

## Start with the value of a point

Points do not have a fixed cash value. A domestic economy reward seat, a long-haul premium cabin, and a gift card can each produce a very different return.

Choose a realistic redemption you would make, then divide its cash price—after subtracting taxes and carrier charges—by the points required. Use that estimate consistently when comparing cards.

## Apply the earn rate to your spending

Check the rate for each major spending category rather than relying on the headline rate. Some cards award fewer points for government payments, utilities, or spending above a monthly cap.

For a simple annual estimate:

1. Group your expected spend by earn category.
2. Multiply each group by its applicable earn rate.
3. Apply any monthly or annual points caps.
4. Multiply the total points by your realistic point value.

## Treat bonus points separately

A sign-up bonus can make the first year look unusually strong. Check the minimum spend, eligible applicant rules, bonus timing, and whether the annual fee is charged before the points arrive.

Compare the ongoing value without the bonus as well. That is the better guide to whether the card remains useful after year one.

## Include transfers and practical constraints

Flexible rewards programs may transfer to several airline partners, but transfer ratios and availability matter. Also consider whether your preferred airline has useful routes from your home airport and whether reward seats are available when you tend to travel.

Finally, subtract the annual fee and any likely surcharges from the value of the points and benefits. The better card is the one that leaves you with more usable value—not necessarily more points.

Credit card rates and promotional terms describe different kinds of debt. Understanding which rate applies—and when—helps you compare the real cost of a card.

## Purchase rate

The purchase rate is the annual interest rate applied to eligible purchases when you do not receive an interest-free period. Interest is usually calculated daily, so carrying a balance can quickly outweigh rewards or cashback.

## Interest-free days

“Up to 55 days interest free” does not mean every purchase gets 55 days. The actual period depends on when in the statement cycle you buy and whether you pay the closing balance in full by the due date.

If you carry a balance, new purchases may lose their interest-free period under the card's terms.

## Balance transfers

A balance transfer moves eligible debt from another card to a new card, often at a promotional rate for a fixed period. Check:

- the transfer fee
- the promotional rate and end date
- the revert rate after the offer ends
- whether purchases receive interest-free days while a transfer remains unpaid

Avoid adding new spending to a balance-transfer card unless you understand how repayments are allocated.

## Cash advances

Cash withdrawals and cash-like transactions can attract a separate fee and a higher rate from the transaction date, with no interest-free period. The definition can include gambling transactions or some money transfers.

For any card, compare fees and rates based on how you expect to use it. If you regularly carry debt, a lower ongoing rate will often matter more than rewards.

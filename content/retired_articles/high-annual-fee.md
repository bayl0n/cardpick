A premium card can justify a high annual fee, but only when its benefits replace spending you would otherwise make. A long list of perks is not the same as a positive return.

## Count benefits at your own value

Start with benefits you are confident you will use: annual travel credits, lounge visits, included insurance, or status benefits. Value each one at what you would have paid—not the provider's advertised price.

For example, a lounge pass has little value if you normally arrive shortly before boarding or rarely travel through an eligible airport.

## Watch for conditions

Travel credits may require booking through a particular portal. Insurance may only activate after a minimum amount of the trip is charged to the card. Dining offers may be limited to selected venues or dates.

Read the eligibility and redemption rules before including a benefit in your calculation.

## Compare the net annual cost

Add the conservative value of benefits you expect to use, then subtract the annual fee and any likely interest or transaction charges. Run the calculation for both the first year and later years because sign-up offers can distort the result.

If the ongoing value is marginal, a lower-fee card is usually the more resilient choice. You should not need to change your normal spending just to recover a card fee.
